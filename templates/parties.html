Rules and Guidelines

Statutory protocols governing candidate campaigning, observer conduct, and election day integrity.

1. Voter Registration Rules for Political Parties ▼

Citizens must be at least 18 years old on qualifying date and ordinarily resident in the respective polling division. Dual or multiple registrations across constituencies are strictly prohibited under electoral law. Accredited political party agents have the statutory right to inspect draft electoral registers during the official claims period and submit formal objections.

2. Campaign Conduct & Expenditure Regulations ▼

All candidates and political parties must strictly adhere to spending ceilings specified under the Regulation of Election Expenditure Act (No. 3 of 2023). A strict 48-hour campaign silence period (blackout) applies prior to polling day. Campaigning using state resources, public vehicles, hate speech, or religious incitement is strictly punishable by law.

3. Polling-Station Agent Rules ▼

Each recognized political party and independent group is entitled to appoint up to 2 polling agents per polling booth. Agents must be registered electors within the administrative district. Polling agents may observe voter check-in, ballot issuance, and the sealing of ballot boxes, but must remain in designated observation areas without interfering with voters or possessing mobile phones inside the booth.

4. Face-Verification Process Explained for Observers ▼

On polling day, the voter presents their NIC/SLIN. The polling booth camera captures a live video frame and extracts a 1:1 mathematical feature embedding. This embedding is matched against the encrypted template registered for that NIC. The entire match is performed locally within milliseconds without transmitting raw images over the network. If a biometric mismatch occurs, the Presiding Officer follows standard challenged voter verification protocols.

Accreditation is granted strictly to registered political parties, independent candidate representatives, and authorized domestic/international monitoring bodies (PAFFREL, CMEV, ANFREL, EU EOM). Observers must abide by the official Code of Conduct for Election Observers.
